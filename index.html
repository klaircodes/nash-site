Admin and roles

“Who is allowed to do what?”

You decide, and it holds for everyone.

There are two kinds of seat. One uses what the other allows.

Members

use what you have allowed — the models you turned on, their own accounts, their own usage against a limit you set.

They cannot share a tool with the team or publish one to the organisation.

Admins

decide what that is — which models are on, what each person can spend, which tools reach everyone.

And they see usage across all twelve people. Usage, never conversations.

Security

Your data stays yours.

Everything here is on by default for organisations. None of it is an add-on, and none of it costs extra.

Encrypted in transit and at rest
Everything, everywhere, by default — not a setting someone has to remember to turn on.
Never used to train models
Not ours, not anyone else's. What your team writes stays your team's.
Full audit logs
Every action attributable to a person, exportable when someone asks.
Single sign-on
Your identity provider, your rules. Nobody gets a second password to lose.
Two-factor sign-in
On by default for organisations, and you can require it for everyone.
Roles and permissions
Decide who can use which models, connect which tools, and share what.

Just as important: what never happens.

No one else reading your chats
Each person’s conversations and memory are private to them, including from admins. Admins see usage, never content.
No shared company key
If you bring your own key, it is encrypted and used only for you. There is no quiet fallback to a shared one when something goes wrong.
No training on your conversations
Not by us, and not by the providers you route to on your own account.
No one at Nash reading your chats
Support cannot browse your workspace. If we need something to help you, we ask.
No lock-in as a retention strategy
The software is open source and the deployment is documented. Staying is a choice you keep making.

About

We think AI should belong to the people using it.

Nash is made by Backboard.

Most teams got to AI the same way: one person expensed a subscription, then another, then four. Now the work is spread across tools that do not know about each other, on accounts nobody can see, with context that disappears every time a tab closes.

Nash is the opposite arrangement. One place, every model, a memory that is yours alone, and controls that belong to the organisation rather than to whoever set it up first — and all of it open source, so nobody has to take our word for how it works.

Open by default
Every line is public under an MIT licence. You can read it, change it, and run it yourself.
Built on Backboard
The memory that carries your context between sessions is Backboard's, ranked first on the leading memory benchmarks.
No lock-in by design
Bring your own keys, export what you have written, or take the whole thing and host it. Leaving stays possible on purpose.
