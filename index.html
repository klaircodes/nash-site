Admin and roles

“Who is allowed to do what?”

You decide, and it holds for everyone.

There are two kinds of seat. One uses what the other allows.

Members

use what you have allowed — the models you turned on, their own connected accounts, and their own usage against a limit you set.

They cannot share a tool with the team or publish one to the organisation. That is not their call.

Admins

decide what that is — which models are on, what each person can spend a month, and which tools reach everyone.

And they see usage across all twelve people, not just their own. Change one person's limit; nobody else notices.

Your data

“Where does our data actually go?”

Where you send it. Nowhere else.

Bring your own provider accounts if you would rather. Your key is encrypted and used for your requests alone — there is no shared company key behind it, nothing trains a model, and your team signs in through your own identity provider.

Security

Your data stays yours.

Everything here is on by default for organisations. None of it is an add-on, and none of it costs extra.

Encrypted in transit and at rest
Everything, everywhere, by default — not a setting someone has to remember to turn on.
Never used to train models
Not ours, not anyone else's. What your team writes stays your team's.
Full audit logs
Every action attributable to a person, exportable when someone asks.

Your identity provider, your rules. Nobody gets a second password to lose.
Two-factor sign-in

About

We think AI should belong to the people using it.

Nash is made by Backboard.

Most teams got to AI the same way: one person expensed a subscription, then another, then four. Now the work is spread across tools that do not know about each other, on accounts nobody can see, with context that disappears every time a tab closes.

Nash is the opposite arrangement. One place, every model, shared context, and controls that belong to the organisation rather than to whoever set it up first — and all of it open source, so nobody has to take our word for how it works.

Open by default
Every line is public under an MIT licence. You can read it, change it, and run it yourself.
Built on Backboard

No lock-in by design
